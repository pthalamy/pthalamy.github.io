Pierre Thalamy

Lead Software Engineer, Platform team at Uptale

I lead the Platform team at Uptale, the SaaS backbone behind an immersive learning product: backend services, cloud infrastructure and the tooling a team needs to ship with confidence. Full-stack by trade, strongest on the backend and infrastructure side, and a go-to engineer for colleagues on the other areas of the product.

Before industry, I completed a PhD on distributed self-reconfiguration algorithms for modular robots, working towards programmable matter at the FEMTO-ST Institute.

    • Own the backend and cloud infrastructure of the Platform area: ASP.NET Core services on Azure, provisioned and versioned with Terraform.
    • Ship full-stack features end to end, from data model and API to the React and TypeScript front end.
    • Serve as a reference engineer beyond my team, contributing to architecture, infrastructure, reliability and security topics across the product.

  2. PhD Researcher in Computer Science

    FEMTO-ST Institute, CNRS, Université Bourgogne Franche-Comté 2017 to 2020

    Research on distributed intelligence for programmable matter: how large ensembles of simple modular micro-robots can coordinate to rearrange themselves into arbitrary 3D shapes, quickly.

    • Designed scaffold-based self-reconfiguration algorithms that bring reconfiguration time down to O(∛N) for classes of shapes, with N the number of modules.
    • Technical lead on VisibleSim, the C++ modular robot simulator used by several research teams worldwide.
    • Five publications in international journals and conferences (AAMAS, IROS, RAS, SWEVO); thesis defended in October 2020.
    • Taught and coordinated undergraduate courses in networking, security and operating systems.
    C++Distributed algorithmsMulti-agent systemsSimulation

Selected work

Uptale platform

The SaaS backbone that ties Uptale's Studio and Playtime experiences together: backend and infrastructure used by large organisations to distribute and track 360° and VR training across headsets, browsers and learning management systems.

.NETAzureTerraform

VisibleSim

Open source C++ simulator for modular robots and programmable matter. I led its development for five years, from a research prototype to a tool used by teams in France, Hong Kong and the United States.

C++OpenGLOpen source

Scaffold-based self-reconfiguration

My doctoral research: a deterministic, fully distributed way for swarms of rotating micro-modules to build the internal scaffold of a shape and coat it, cutting reconfiguration time by orders of magnitude.

Distributed algorithmsSwarm robotics

Meld virtual machine

An embedded virtual machine written in C for Meld, the declarative language of the Claytronics project at Carnegie Mellon, so that logic programs could run directly on resource-constrained robot modules.
